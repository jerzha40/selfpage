## I did not begin with a map.

I began with a Fourier transform.

During my first year as a mathematics undergraduate, I tried to implement numerical algorithms in C++. I was learning what it meant for a mathematical formula to become a program—and learning Git badly enough that parts of the work were never properly preserved.

What followed was not a carefully designed academic trajectory. I encountered physics-informed neural networks, worked on problems involving geometry and mesh generation, and later spent a great deal of time on private experiments in automated financial systems.

Some directions were chosen for me. Some were my own. Several remain unfinished.

During a summer exchange at the University of Alberta, I explored computational problems involving differential equations, stochastic dynamics, and financial models.

Today, as a graduate student, I am trying to approach inverse problems with greater mathematical care: what can we infer about a system from the observations it leaves behind?

This site is not a retrospective claim that everything was part of a grand plan. It is an attempt to preserve the work, recognize what I have actually learned, and turn unfinished experiments into things another person can understand and examine.

I am still building.
